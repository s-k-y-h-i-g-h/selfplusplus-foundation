---

## What Is Metformin?

Metformin is a first-line treatment for type 2 diabetes, approved by the FDA in 1994. It's one of the most studied drugs in human history, with decades of safety data and millions of users worldwide.

Beyond diabetes, metformin has emerged as a leading candidate for longevity intervention. Its mechanisms overlap with known longevity pathways (AMPK, mTOR, mitochondrial function), and observational data suggests diabetic patients on metformin may live longer than non-diabetics not on the drug.

---

## How It Works

Metformin's longevity mechanisms are complex and not fully understood:

### Primary Mechanisms
- **AMPK activation** - Mimics calorie restriction, improves metabolic health
- **mTOR inhibition** - Reduces protein synthesis, promotes autophagy
- **Mitochondrial complex I inhibition** - Mild stress response (hormesis)
- **Reduced hepatic glucose production** - Improves insulin sensitivity

### Secondary Effects
- **Reduced inflammation** - Lower CRP, IL-6 levels
- **Improved gut microbiome** - Changes in microbial composition
- **Reduced IGF-1 signaling** - Similar to growth hormone restriction
- **Epigenetic effects** - Potential impacts on DNA methylation clocks

## The Evidence

### Human Studies

| Study | Population | Finding | Limitations |
|-------|------------|---------|-------------|
| **UKPDS (1998)** | Type 2 diabetics | Reduced cardiovascular mortality | Diabetes population only |
| **Observational meta-analyses** | Diabetics on metformin | Lower all-cause mortality vs. other diabetes drugs | Confounding by indication |
| **TAME Trial (planned)** | Non-diabetics, 65-79 | Primary longevity outcome trial | Not yet completed |
| **Various cohort studies** | General population | Mixed results on longevity benefit | Observational, confounding |

**Key Finding:** Diabetic patients on metformin often show lower mortality than non-diabetics not on metformin. This "metformin paradox" suggests potential longevity benefits beyond glucose control.

### Animal Studies

- **C. elegans:** 10-20% lifespan extension in multiple studies
- **Mice:** Mixed results; some studies show lifespan extension, others show no effect or reduced lifespan in young animals
- **Mechanistic consistency:** AMPK activation, mTOR inhibition, autophagy induction consistently observed

### Preclinical

- Strong evidence for AMPK activation and mTOR inhibition
- Clear effects on mitochondrial function
- Well-characterized impact on insulin/IGF-1 signaling
- Epigenetic clock effects observed in cell cultures

## Practical Considerations

### Potential Benefits
- Well-established safety profile (50+ years of use)
- Inexpensive (generic, widely available)
- Multiple mechanisms overlapping with known longevity pathways
- Potential cancer prevention effects (observational data)
- Cardiovascular benefits in diabetic populations
- Possible cognitive benefits

### Potential Risks
- **GI side effects** - Nausea, diarrhea, abdominal discomfort (common initially)
- **B12 deficiency** - Long-term use associated with reduced B12 absorption
- **Lactic acidosis** - Rare but serious; risk increased in kidney/liver disease
- **Muscle mass loss** - Some evidence of reduced muscle protein synthesis
- **Blunted exercise adaptations** - May reduce some benefits of exercise

### Who Might Benefit
- Older adults (65+) with metabolic dysfunction
- Individuals with insulin resistance or prediabetes
- Those seeking low-cost, well-studied longevity intervention
- People with family history of diabetes/cancer

### Who Should Avoid
- Individuals with kidney disease (eGFR < 30)
- Those with liver disease
- People with history of lactic acidosis
- Those with acute heart failure
- Individuals planning intense endurance training (may blunt adaptations)

## Our Position

**Metformin is the most well-studied, accessible, and potentially beneficial longevity intervention available today.**

However:
- Evidence is strongest for diabetic populations
- Non-diabetic longevity benefits remain unproven in randomized trials
- The TAME trial (Targeting Aging with Metformin) will provide definitive answers
- Individual risk/benefit must be evaluated by a healthcare professional

**For self++ beneficiaries:** We prioritize metformin access for those with demonstrated metabolic dysfunction or diabetes. Longevity use in healthy individuals is not currently prioritized due to limited evidence.
