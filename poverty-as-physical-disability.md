## The Core Argument

Poverty is not a choice. It is a **physical disability** caused by biological changes that make it harder to escape poverty. These changes are invisible, intergenerational, and self-reinforcing.

---

## The Science

### Epigenetic Changes from Poverty

Epigenetics is the study of how gene expression changes without altering DNA sequence. Environmental conditions — including poverty — can cause epigenetic modifications that affect biology across generations.

**Documented changes from chronic poverty exposure:**

| Change | Mechanism | Effect |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| Altered cortisol regulation | Chronic stress → HPA axis dysregulation | Impaired stress response, reduced cognitive function |
| Metabolic changes | Nutritional deprivation → gene expression shifts | Higher risk of diabetes, cardiovascular disease |
| Cognitive impairment | Chronic stress + malnutrition → brain development changes | Reduced working memory, executive function |
| Immune dysregulation | Chronic inflammation → immune system changes | Higher susceptibility to disease |
| Telomere shortening | Accelerated cellular aging from stress | Premature aging, reduced lifespan |

### Intergenerational Transmission

These changes don't stop with the individual. Research shows epigenetic modifications can be transmitted to offspring through:

- **In utero exposure** — maternal stress affects fetal development
- **Germ cell modification** — epigenetic changes in sperm/eggs
- **Postnatal environment** — continued stress exposure reinforces patterns

A child born into poverty is not starting from zero. They are starting with a **biological deficit** inherited from their parents' poverty exposure.

## The Disability Framework

### Why This Matters

If poverty creates physical changes that impair function, then poverty is a **disability** — not a character flaw.

**Analogy:** You wouldn't tell someone in a wheelchair to "just walk harder." You wouldn't tell someone with a broken leg to "just run faster." You provide **accessibility infrastructure** — ramps, prosthetics, rehabilitation.

**The same logic applies to poverty:** You don't tell someone with epigenetic poverty damage to "just get a job." You provide **biological accessibility infrastructure** — healthspan interventions, nutrition, stress reduction, cognitive support.

### The Invisible Disability

The key insight: poverty-induced epigenetic changes are **invisible**. Unlike a broken leg, you can't see:
- Altered cortisol regulation
- Impaired cognitive function from chronic stress
- Metabolic changes from nutritional deprivation
- Immune dysregulation from chronic inflammation

This invisibility is why poverty is misunderstood as laziness or lack of willpower. The disability is real — it's just not visible.

## Policy Implications

### What "Accessibility Infrastructure" Means

If poverty is a physical disability, then the solution isn't just economic transfers. It's **biological intervention**:

1. **Universal healthspan access** — Longevity interventions for everyone, not just the rich
2. **Nutritional sovereignty** — Access to high-quality nutrition, not just calories
3. **Stress reduction infrastructure** — Mental health support, community building, safe environments
4. **Cognitive support** — Educational and developmental interventions that account for biological differences
5. **Intergenerational repair** — Interventions that break the cycle of epigenetic transmission

### The Healthspan Gap

Currently, longevity and healthspan interventions are concentrated among the wealthy:
- Supplements, biohacking, anti-aging therapies
- High-quality nutrition, fitness facilities
- Stress-reducing environments, mental health support

The poor don't have access to these. The result: **healthspan inequality compounds wealth inequality**. The rich live longer AND better. The poor die younger AND worse.

This is the core challenge of accessible longevity: making healthspan interventions available to everyone, not just those who can afford them.
