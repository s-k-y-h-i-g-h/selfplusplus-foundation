## The Core Argument

War is not random. It follows patterns. One of the deepest patterns is biological: poverty creates epigenetic damage that makes populations more vulnerable to conflict, and conflict creates more poverty, which creates more epigenetic damage. This is a self-perpetuating cycle that operates at the civilizational level.

## The Self-Perpetuating Cycle

Step 1: Poverty → Epigenetic Damage
Step 2: Epigenetic Damage → Reduced Economic Productivity
Step 3: Reduced Resources → Increased Competition
Step 4: Competition → War
Step 5: War → More Epigenetic Damage
Step 6: More Epigenetic Damage → More Poverty

This is not a metaphor. This is a biological feedback loop.

## The Evidence

### Syrian Refugee Study (Nature, 2025)
Epigenetic signatures of intergenerational exposure to violence in three generations of Syrian refugees. DNA methylation patterns transmitted across three generations.

### BMJ Commentary (February 2026)
Intergenerational Epigenetic Imprints of War. Violence leaves epigenetic marks that transmit across generations.

### PMC Review (2018)
Intergenerational Transmission of Disadvantage. Poverty creates epigenetic changes that pass to offspring.
